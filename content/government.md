## The map on your site locks out one in five constituents

Federal, state, and local agencies publish thousands of ArcGIS maps every year. By default, more than one in five constituents cannot use them. Blind, low-vision, colorblind, deafblind, mobility-impaired, low-literacy, autistic, and cognitively disabled users are all locked out, and most agency maps fail [Section 508 (Opens an external website)](https://www.section508.gov/buy/), the [2024 DOJ ADA Title II rule (Opens an external website)](https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/), and [Section 504 obligations on grant recipients (Opens an external website)](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/ocr/civilrights/resources/factsheets/504.pdf).

A blind constituent loading a planning map, a flood-risk map, a benefits-locator map, or a polling-place map hears “blank.” A low-vision user cannot zoom past 200% without labels overlapping into noise. A colorblind user cannot tell a hazard zone from its surrounding terrain. A user with limited hand mobility cannot operate pan-and-pinch gestures. A low-literacy or cognitively disabled user cannot extract meaning from a dense legend. An autistic user is overwhelmed by visual clutter and cluttered color choices a non-autistic user filters out without thinking.

Turn-by-turn directions, AI summaries, and data tables are not a fix. They strip out the spatial relationships that let a map answer questions a list cannot, like the shape of the building on the top-left corner of the map.

## What happens if the map gap is still open in April 2027

The [2024 DOJ ADA Title II rule (Opens an external website)](https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/) requires every state and local government, and every public entity that contracts with one, to bring digital content to [WCAG 2.1 AA (Opens an external website)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/) by April 2027. Federal agencies have been bound by [Section 508 (Opens an external website)](https://www.section508.gov/buy/) since 1998, and by [Section 504 (Opens an external website)](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/ocr/civilrights/resources/factsheets/504.pdf) on grant recipients since 1973. Maps published on agency websites, kiosks, and indoor wayfinding systems are squarely in scope.

-   [Digital accessibility lawsuits rose 24% in 2025 (Opens an external website)](https://www.ecomback.com/annual-2025-ada-website-accessibility-lawsuit-report). [Legal fees can be $350,000 per complaint (Opens an external website)](https://www.nascio.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/NASCIO_The-Economic-Case-for-Digital-Accessibility_2025_a11y.pdf).
-   Demand letters arrive in volume. [Each one costs roughly $25,000 to respond to (Opens an external website)](https://testparty.ai/blog/ada-demand-letter-breakdown), and one agency can receive many.
-   Federal grant recipients risk [Section 504 findings (Opens an external website)](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/ocr/civilrights/resources/factsheets/504.pdf) that put future funding at risk.
-   Every month a complaint sits open is a month a constituent who needed the map could not use it.

The map is the easiest thing on the site to point at and the hardest thing to retrofit. Lawyers and users will find it first.
